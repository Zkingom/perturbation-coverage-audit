# Abstract (draft v0.2, 2026-10-28)

Models of single-cell perturbation responses are increasingly used to decide which perturbations to test, yet their uncertainty statements are usually validated only on average. We audited split-conformal prediction sets for perturbation-response models in genome-scale CRISPRi Perturb-seq screens, treating the target gene as the exchangeable unit and holding out whole transcription-start-site groups. Marginal coverage was nominal, but coverage of the 20% of perturbations with the strongest measured effects was about one half in pre-registered confirmations in a second cell line (RPE1; 0.48, 95% CI 0.43–0.53) and on target genes outside the essential-gene library (0.52, 95% CI 0.49–0.55). We show that this value is largely fixed by construction: the conformity score is close to a multiple of measured effect strength, and quantile-stratified coverage of such a score is one half in the top quintile. We provide this constructive reference and the scale-independent share of the score as the quantities against which stratified coverage should be read. In a post-confirmation test, GEARS was more accurate than a linear baseline but showed no detectable increase in that share. A deployable proxy for effect scale built from prior-knowledge neighbours met the pre-registered criteria on RPE1 (+9.7 percentage points (95% CI 5.8 to 13.6) top-quintile coverage at matched width) and did not meet them on the non-library genes (+1.0 percentage points (95% CI -0.1 to 2.1); pre-registered margin 5 percentage points).
