# Results (draft v0.1, 2026-10-12; development set and RPE1 only — GWPS pending)

*Draft for internal review. All development-set numbers carry selection optimism (methods and thresholds were chosen on the same units). The GWPS secondary confirmation has not been run.*

## Marginally valid split-conformal intervals systematically under-cover the strongest perturbations

We calibrated perturbation-level prediction sets for a linear low-rank perturbation model (linear-PCA; score S1, the σ-scaled RMS error on the 50 most perturbation-specific genes) with split conformal prediction at α = 0.1, treating each target gene as the exchangeable unit and holding out whole TSS groups (repeated grouped 7-fold, 3 rounds, 21 splits). Marginal coverage was at its nominal level (development 0.90; RPE1 0.896). Coverage, however, collapsed in the top quintile of measured effect strength (tier T5): 0.516 in the K562 essential development set and 0.480 (95% CI 0.431–0.528; 1,051 test rows, 1,751 genes) in the pre-registered RPE1 confirmation set, with tiers T1–T4 at ≥0.99 in both (Fig. 1a,b). The same pattern held for a mean-response model and for a GO-embedding linear model (RPE1 T5 under M0: mean model 0.493 at nominal α; linear-GO 0.504 width-matched). Because S1 for the mean model equals the true effect strength, under-coverage of strong perturbations is partly a consequence of how prediction error scales with effect size; we therefore treat effect strength as the explanatory variable for the failure rather than as a novel finding (effect-size dependence of prediction difficulty was reported by Li et al., *Sci. Adv.* 2026, doi:10.1126/sciadv.aed3414).

## Similarity- and uncertainty-based difficulty proxies do not track this failure axis

We compared deployable difficulty proxies under randomised width matching to M0 (same expected mean interval width). On RPE1, none of six distance- or model-uncertainty-based proxies improved T5 coverage (Fig. 1c): similarity/training-quality difficulty (normalised +1.7 pp (95% CI -0.1 to 3.6); Mondrian -1.2 pp (95% CI -4.1 to 1.6)), Mondrian on raw GO-distance quintiles (-1.1 pp (95% CI -3.7 to 1.4)) or STRING-distance quintiles (+0.1 pp (95% CI -3.5 to 3.7)), and a PRESCRIBE-like bootstrap model-uncertainty proxy (normalised -4.6 pp (95% CI -9.7 to 0.6); Mondrian -1.8 pp (95% CI -3.4 to -0.2)). The development-set advantage of the Mondrian similarity proxy (+5.7 pp) did not replicate. We note that this bootstrap proxy represents one implementation for a linear low-rank model and does not reproduce PRESCRIBE itself.

## A proxy built from prior-network neighbours partially repairs coverage of strong perturbations

A deployable proxy ĥ of effect strength, learned from prior-knowledge neighbours (GO and STRING) of each target using measured responses of training perturbations only, predicted effect strength out of sample (RPE1 Spearman ρ = 0.43, 95% CI 0.39–0.47; AUROC for T5 0.74). Normalising the conformity score by ĥ (M1) raised width-matched T5 coverage on RPE1 by +9.7 pp (95% CI 5.8 to 13.6), versus +12.0 pp (95% CI 8.0 to 16.0) in the development set (Fig. 1c). In the pre-specified decision scenario (top 10% of perturbations by ĥ) coverage rose by +17.4 pp (95% CI 12.6 to 22.3), with a subset interval-width ratio of 1.38; because this subset is selected by the same ĥ that allocates width, this endpoint shows that M1 reallocates a fixed total width toward perturbations ranked high by ĥ. The pre-registered hierarchical criteria were met on RPE1 (step 1 vs M0; step 2 vs the pre-specified strongest development-set baseline, +10.9 pp (95% CI 7.8 to 14.0)), with all guards satisfied (nominal marginal coverage 0.900; permuted-ĥ control mean -5.2 pp, 0/20 permutations meeting the step-1 criterion). We stress that step 2 passed partly because the comparator failed to replicate, and that the repair is partial: M1 T5 coverage was 0.577 on RPE1, far below nominal, while an oracle normalised by true effect strength reached 0.999; coverage in T4 decreased from 1.000 to 0.953. A score fitted to the same features (Mscore-N; +11.9 pp (95% CI 7.8 to 16.0)) performed similarly, indicating that the gain derives from the prior-neighbour features rather than from the specific normalisation. Results were consistent under grid-based width matching (step 1 +10.3 pp (95% CI 6.3 to 14.2)), alternative gene-set sizes (k = 20, 200), split-half tier assignment, and for linear-GO.

## Confounding

In a logistic model of M0 miscoverage, log effect strength retained a strong positive association after adjustment for split-half noise and reliability, cell number, GO and STRING distance, target baseline expression, GO similarity and shared neighbours (split-half version, RPE1: 4.50 → 3.63 log-odds per SD; development 5.30 → 5.21). Distance terms showed no robust independent association for linear-PCA, whereas for linear-GO, GO distance retained a modest positive association in both datasets; the claim that distance does not capture the failure axis is therefore restricted to the primary model. Target baseline expression was robustly negatively associated with miscoverage in both datasets (mechanism not established).

## Biological characterisation (development set, descriptive)

Perturbations persistently miscovered across models were essentially the strongest-effect perturbations (78 of 93 core genes were among the 93 highest-effect genes) and were enriched for RNA exosome, nuclear RNA decay, RNA 3′-end processing, RNA polymerase II/TFIID, ribosome and rRNA processing terms. Strong-effect (T5) targets were more often CORUM complex members (78% vs 64%; OR 1.96, p = 6×10⁻⁷) and had more STRING partners; after adjustment for STRING degree and GO annotation count, complex membership was no longer independently associated (+0.10 log-odds per SD, 95% CI −0.04 to 0.24), which we interpret cautiously given the overlap between STRING database evidence and CORUM and annotation bias toward well-studied genes.

## Limitations stated in the protocol

- 82% of RPE1 E-set targets (1,444/1,751) overlap the development set; the RPE1 confirmation tests generalisation across cell lines rather than across genes. Generalisation to unseen genes will be tested in the GWPS secondary confirmation (targets outside the K562 essential set).
- All datasets come from one laboratory and platform (Replogle et al. 2022).
- Tier assignment uses split-specific references; 2.7% (development) and 3.0% (RPE1) of genes change tier between rounds.
- The linear models' hyperparameters K and d reached the upper grid bound in all 21 RPE1 splits; a post-confirmation exploratory analysis with an extended grid is planned.
