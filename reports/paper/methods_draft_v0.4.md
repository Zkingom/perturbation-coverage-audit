# Methods (draft v0.4, 2026-10-28)

**Evidence tiers.** Results are labelled as **confirmatory** (a pre-registered endpoint of a protocol frozen with SHA-256 manifests before any response of that confirmation set entered a score, computed once in the sense defined under *What was computed when*), **descriptive** (a pre-specified quantity without a pre-registered decision rule), **post-confirmation exploratory** (its own protocol frozen before running, but after the confirmations were unblinded) or **post hoc illustrative** (outside every protocol, added in response to a specific question). No confirmatory configuration, criterion or classification was changed after it was observed; a complete freeze timeline, the first read of each dataset's responses, and every post-freeze deviation with its nature and validation are given in Supplementary Table S3.

*Internal draft. Values are copied from the frozen result files named in brackets; protocol documents and hash manifests are in `reports/protocol/` and `manifests/`.*

## Data

We used the genome-scale CRISPRi Perturb-seq screens of Replogle et al.¹ (raw single-cell and bulk files, Figshare+ doi:10.25452/figshare.plus.20029387.v1): K562 essential-gene screen (development set), RPE1 essential-gene screen (primary confirmation set) and the K562 genome-wide screen (secondary confirmation set) from which all 2,057 targets of the K562 essential-gene screen were removed before any other processing step, so that no such target enters any fold including training; we call the remainder the "non-library target set" and note that it is defined by absence from that screening library, not by a biological definition of non-essentiality. File sizes and checksums were verified against the publisher's records (computed MD5 where provided, uploader-supplied MD5 otherwise) and SHA-256 values were recorded. For each perturbation we formed a pseudobulk profile as the mean over cells of log1p(counts per 10,000) and, for split-half analyses, two pseudobulks from a random partition of its cells into halves A and B (fixed seed). Single-cell pseudobulks were checked against the publisher's bulk files (per-perturbation mean UMI; non-library target set maximum absolute difference 2.4 × 10⁻⁵, zero elements outside tolerance).

**Units and leakage control.** The exchangeable unit is the target gene. Evaluation units ("E units") were targets with at least 30 cells that met the pre-specified inclusion rules; targets sharing a transcription start site within 1 kb (Ensembl GRCh38 coordinates) were merged into TSS groups, because CRISPRi guides can repress neighbouring promoters. All splits assign whole TSS groups; non-E perturbations were always placed in training ("always-train") unless they shared a TSS group with an E unit, in which case they were dropped. Sizes: development 1,786 E units (1,721 TSS groups); RPE1 1,751 genes evaluated; non-library target set 5,683 E units (5,449 TSS groups) plus 2,001 always-train units.

**Cell-count floor applies to the reference, not to the fitted models.** Evaluation units require ≥30 cells. Inside a split, the reference quantities μ_tr and σ_g — and therefore the mean-response model, whose prediction is exactly μ_tr — are computed only from training-pool units with ≥30 cells (`pipeline_v05.MIN_CELLS_REF = 30`, asserted at run time). The linear models' fitting columns and the neighbour features of the deployable proxies (ĥ's GO- and STRING-weighted neighbour effect strength, and the neighbour cell-count/noise features) carry **no** cell-count floor, so responses measured in fewer than 30 cells do enter the gene embeddings, the fitted coefficients and the neighbour averages. The asymmetry is deliberate for the reference (which must be stable) but was not imposed on the predictors; it means that part of the proxy's input is noisier than the reference it is compared against. A sensitivity analysis weighting neighbour features by cell number, or applying the same floor to them, was not run and is stated as future work.

**Splits.** Repeated grouped 7-fold: in each of 3 rounds (seed = base + round), TSS groups were dealt to 7 balanced test folds; for each test fold the remaining units were split by TSS group into validation (10% of E), calibration (15%) and training (rest), giving 21 splits in which every E unit is tested once per round. Split files were written once, set read-only, and verified by hash before every run.

## Perturbation-response models

Three models were trained on the training pool (E training units plus always-train units): (i) the **mean** model, predicting the training-reference centroid; (ii) **linear-PCA**, the linear baseline of Ahlmann-Eltze et al.² (gene embedding from the top K principal components of the training expression matrix, two-sided ridge λ = 0.1), with K selected on the validation fold from {5, 10, 20, 30, 50}; and (iii) **linear-GO**, the same model with perturbation embeddings from a d-dimensional adjacency spectral embedding of the GEARS Gene Ontology graph³, d ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 50, 100}. linear-PCA with k = 50 was the pre-specified primary model. No deep model was evaluated (Discussion).

**Reference and score.** Following Systema⁴, expression was referenced to the centroid of training perturbations rather than to control cells: μ is the mean over training perturbations with ≥ 30 cells, σ_g = 1.4826 × MAD_g, floored at the 5th percentile across genes. The nonconformity score S1 of a perturbation is the σ-scaled root-mean-square prediction error over the k = 50 genes with the largest absolute specific effect |y − μ|/σ, excluding readout columns of the target gene (k = 20 and 200 as sensitivity analyses). **Effect strength** E_true is S1 of the mean model, i.e. the σ-scaled RMS specific effect on those genes; tiers T1–T5 are its quintiles over the E units of each dataset.

## Conformal calibration methods

All methods target 1 − α = 0.9 and use the calibration fold of each split.
- **M0**: split conformal prediction⁵ with the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉-th calibration score.
- **M1** (primary): normalised score S1/(ĥ + c), c = γ · median(ĥ_OOF), γ = 0 selected on the development set, where ĥ is a deployable prediction of effect strength (below).
- **Distance / difficulty baselines**: Mdist-N and Mdist-M (normalised and Mondrian [3 bins] calibration on a similarity/training-quality difficulty proxy d̂); MD-GO and MD-STR (Mondrian calibration on quintiles of the raw GO-Jaccard or STRING distance to the training pool); Munc-N and Munc-M (a PRESCRIBE-like model-uncertainty proxy: the dispersion of linear-PCA predictions over 50 bootstrap refits of the training pool). We call the latter "PRESCRIBE-like" and do not claim to reproduce PRESCRIBE⁶.
- **Secondary and reference methods**: Mscore-N (all ĥ features fitted directly to the out-of-fold score), M2 (Mondrian on ĥ), M3 (conditional conformal of Gibbs, Cherian and Candès⁷, own exact linear-programming implementation cross-checked against the authors' package), a **reference method** that normalises by the measured E_true (not deployable, and partly definitional: for the mean-response model the score equals E_true exactly, so the normalised score is constant, and on the non-library target set the normalised score of linear-PCA is nearly constant, coefficient of variation 0.008) and **M_perm** (ĥ permuted within calibration and test; 20 permutations; negative control).

**Deployable proxies.** ĥ is a ridge regression (λ chosen by grouped inner cross-validation) of log E_true on prior-only and training-derived features: GO-Jaccard and STRING similarities and distances to the training pool, Jaccard- and STRING-weighted mean effect strengths of the 20 most GO-similar and all STRING neighbours in the training pool, number of GO annotations and STRING degree. The learner is trained on the E training units of the split only (always-train units act as neighbours and in model fitting), with 5 inner TSS-group folds for out-of-fold features. No feature uses the response, cell number or knockdown of a calibration or test unit.

### Calibration methods compared

Notation for one split: calibration scores S^cal = (S_1..S_{n_cal}), test units with proxies, and q̂_α(·) the split-conformal quantile, i.e. the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉-th smallest value of its argument (+∞ if that index exceeds n; `methods_v06.qhat`). Prediction sets are {y : S(x,y) ≤ t_i}; the reported width is the capped radius (Width matching, below). Every method below uses only calibration scores and proxies; no test response enters any threshold.

| name | family | proxy | threshold for test unit i |
|---|---|---|---|
| M0 | global | — | t_i = q̂_α(S^cal) |
| M1 | normalised | ĥ (predicted effect strength) | t_i = q̂_α(S^cal_j /(ĥ_j+c)) · (ĥ_i+c), c = γ·median(ĥ^oof) |
| M2 | Mondrian | ĥ | t_i = q̂_α({S^cal_j : b(ĥ_j)=b(ĥ_i)}), bins b from quantiles of ĥ^oof |
| M3 | conditional | ĥ | randomised Gibbs–Cherian–Candès set for Φ(ĥ) = span{1, z, z²} or natural cubic splines (df 3, 5) |
| Mdist-N | normalised | d̂ (similarity / training-quality difficulty) | as M1 with ĥ → d̂ |
| Mdist-M | Mondrian | d̂ | as M2 with ĥ → d̂ |
| MD-GO | Mondrian | D_GO (raw GO distance) | as M2 with ĥ → D_GO |
| MD-STR | Mondrian | D_STRING (raw STRING distance) | as M2 with ĥ → D_STRING |
| Munc-N | normalised | σ̂_boot (model's own prediction dispersion) | as M1 with ĥ → σ̂_boot |
| Munc-M | Mondrian | σ̂_boot | as M2 with ĥ → σ̂_boot |
| Mscore-N | normalised | ŝ (all ĥ features refitted to the out-of-fold score) | as M1 with ĥ → ŝ |
| Mcomb-N | normalised | σ̂_comb = exp(OLS of log out-of-fold score on [1, log ĥ, log d̂]) | as M1 with ĥ → σ̂_comb |
| Mcomb-3 | conditional | (ĥ, d̂) | M3 with Φ = span{1, z(log ĥ), z(log d̂)} |
| reference method | normalised | measured effect strength E | as M1 with ĥ → E (not deployable; see Results) |
| M_perm | negative control | permuted ĥ | any of the above with ĥ permuted independently within the calibration set and within the test set |

Implementation details that a reader needs:

1. **Bin edges and normalisation constants never use calibration or test units.** `mondrian_edges` takes quantiles of the *training out-of-fold* proxy, and c = γ·median of the same out-of-fold vector, with γ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1} (`methods_v06.mondrian_edges`, `thresholds`).
2. **M3** (`methods_v06.m3_threshold`) implements the randomised conditional set of Gibbs, Cherian & Candès (2025) exactly, as a single linear program: with g(e) = max{Σᵢ ηᵢ Sᵢ : ηᵢ ∈ [−α, 1−α], Σᵢ ηᵢ φᵢ = −e φ_i}, the test dual satisfies {S : η_{n+1}(S) < U} = {S ≤ −g′(U)}, and −g′(U) is read from the equality-constraint marginals of that LP. U_i = u_i − α with u_i ~ Uniform(0,1) fixed per split by seed. Infeasible programs mean U lies outside the attainable range of η_{n+1} and give ±∞ (all / empty); two documented numerical fallbacks (interior-point, then bisection on η_{n+1}) compute the same quantity. The implementation was cross-checked against `conditionalconformal` 0.0.5 (protocol v0.6.1 §11c).
3. **σ̂_boot** (`ext_v07dev.compute_ext`, `_unc_from_moments`): B = 50 refits of linear-PCA on TSS-group bootstrap resamples of the training pool; per unit, the σ-scaled prediction dispersion (variance with ddof = 1) averaged over the 50 genes with the largest |mean prediction|, target column(s) excluded. Out-of-fold values for training units use only refits in which the unit was out of bag (minimum 3).
4. **D_GO, D_STRING** (`pipeline_v05.distances`): prior-only distances from the query unit to the training pool, identical to the covariates used elsewhere (asserted against the stage-1 cache at run time); out-of-fold versions for training units use the inner folds.
5. **M_perm** (`methods_v06.permute_within`, `eval_v06.N_PERM = 20`): the proxy is permuted within calibration and within test separately, 20 permutations with fixed seeds; the decision subset is always selected with the real ĥ, so the control changes only the calibration, not the subset.
6. **Width matching** (`confirm_v07.rand_rows`): for each method the level α′ is chosen on the grid 0.010–0.400 (step 0.005) so that the capped mean width equals M0's; the two bracketing grid points are mixed with a per-unit uniform draw (probability π) to hit the target in expectation. Statuses `unmatched_too_narrow` / `unmatched_too_wide` record methods whose width cannot reach M0's anywhere on the grid (this is the case for the reference method; Supplementary Table S1). After matching, the nominal level is α′, not α; matching is an **empirical equal-width comparison** and does not by itself confer a finite-sample guarantee at any level (see Width matching).

**Width matching.** Because conditional-coverage gains can be bought with wider sets, methods were compared at equal mean width: for each split, α of each method was chosen on a grid (0.01–0.40, step 0.005) and randomised between the two adjacent grid values so that the expected mean (capped) width equals that of M0 at α = 0.1 (primary, "randomised matching"; nearest-grid matching as sensitivity). The α′ chosen for a method uses only calibration scores, the proxies and the matching target; test responses enter solely in the coverage indicator. Two consequences are stated explicitly: after matching, the theoretical marginal guarantee of a method is 1 − α′, not 1 − 0.1 (on RPE1, M1 matched at α′ between 0.060 and 0.115), and because α′ depends on the realised widths it is an evaluation device for comparing how methods allocate a fixed width budget, not a coverage guarantee at 1 − 0.1. Marginal coverage after matching is therefore reported as a measured quantity. Interval width is the capped radius of the score ball (+∞ replaced by twice the largest calibration score, empty sets counted as 0), a convention fixed in the protocol to accommodate the infinite thresholds that the conditional-conformal method can produce. The reference method that normalises by measured effect strength could not be width-matched: at the lowest α of the grid its expected width was still below the M0 target in 20 of 21 RPE1 and all 21 non-library-target-set and development splits, so its coverage is reported at narrower-than-M0 width and never as a width-matched gain (per-split α′, expected width, realised width ratio and matching status: Supplementary Table S1).

## Pre-registered confirmatory analysis (protocol v0.7)

All selections (model, k, primary method, hyper-parameters, the strongest baseline = Mdist-M, thresholds) were made on the development set and frozen with SHA-256 manifests before any confirmation-set response was read. Endpoints: **E1**, the difference in T5 coverage between a method and its comparator; **E2**, the coverage difference in a decision scenario (the 10% of test perturbations with the highest ĥ), always reported with the subset width ratio. Differences and 95% CIs come from paired generalised estimating equations with TSS-group clusters (*Interval estimation*, below). **B0** (failure present): M0 T5 coverage point estimate < 0.85 and upper 95% CI bound < 0.90. **Step 1** (M1 vs M0) passes if, for both E1 and E2, the lower CI bound is > 0 and the estimate ≥ 5 pp; **step 2** (M1 vs Mdist-M; hierarchical, tested only after step 1) uses a 3 pp margin. Guards: nominal marginal coverage within [0.88, 0.92]; M_perm mean E1 ≤ 2 pp with at most one permutation meeting the step-1 rule; ĥ predictability not in the pre-specified "P−" class. Classifications were taken verbatim from pre-written wording. Power at half the development effect was computed from n_E alone before responses were read.

### Statistical inference

**Interval estimation.** Let the analysis set be one (method, model, k, matching mode) combination. Each row is one *test unit*: one target gene in one split, so a gene contributes up to 21 rows (3 rounds × 7 folds, each gene a test unit once per round) and rows of the same gene are not independent. Write y_{gs} ∈ {0,1} for the coverage indicator of gene g in split s, and c(g) for the TSS group of g.

*Coverage per tier* (`analysis_v06.gee_tiers`). For each effect-strength quintile separately we fit an intercept-only GEE

  g(E[y_{gs}]) = β₀,  g = logit, marginal variance from the binomial family, working correlation = independence, clusters = TSS group c(g),

with `statsmodels.api.GEE(..., family=Binomial(), cov_struct=Independence())`. All rows of the same TSS group — including the same gene's rows across splits and different genes sharing a TSS group — are one cluster, so the sandwich (Liang–Zeger) covariance absorbs both the within-gene repetition and the within-TSS-group dependence. The reported coverage is expit(β̂₀) and the interval expit(β̂₀ ± 1.96·SE(β̂₀)) with the sandwich SE. The independence working correlation makes the point estimate numerically equal to the pooled mean, so only the interval depends on the GEE. When a tier is all-covered or none-covered (β̂₀ not finite), we report the pooled proportion with a cluster bootstrap interval instead (below) and flag the row as `method = "bootstrap (separation)"`.

*Paired coverage difference* (`analysis_v06.paired_T5`). For a method m versus the reference M0 in one tier, the two arms contain the *same* (split, gene) rows (asserted in code). We stack them, add an arm indicator m_i ∈ {0,1}, and fit the same logistic GEE with design [1, m] and the same clusters. The difference and its interval are obtained by the delta method on the two fitted probabilities p₁ = expit(β̂₀+β̂₁), p₀ = expit(β̂₀):

  Δ̂ = p₁ − p₀,  ∇ = [p₁(1−p₁) − p₀(1−p₀),  p₁(1−p₁)],  SE(Δ̂) = √(∇ᵀ V̂ ∇),

with V̂ the sandwich covariance of (β̂₀, β̂₁); the interval is Δ̂ ± 1.96·SE(Δ̂) and the two-sided p-value 2Φ(−|Δ̂|/SE). If either arm is all-covered or none-covered we refit with an identity link (`family=Gaussian()`), in which case Δ̂ = β̂₁ directly; such rows are flagged `how = "identity-link GEE (separation)"`. E1 is this quantity in the top quintile; E2 is the same quantity on the decision subset (P-2 below).

*Cluster bootstrap* (`analysis_v06.cluster_boot`). TSS groups are resampled with replacement (2,000 draws unless stated), the statistic is recomputed on each resampled frame, and the interval is the 2.5/97.5 percentiles. This is used for separated tiers, for the confounding analysis, and wherever a statistic is not a single coverage probability.

*What the interval covers.* These intervals quantify sampling variability of the coverage estimate over genes/TSS groups and splits. They are not conformal guarantees, and they are not valid for comparisons selected after inspecting the same rows; the pre-registered endpoints were fixed before the confirmation data were scored.

**Constructive reference (post-confirmation, descriptive).** If the score S is a strictly increasing function of the stratifying variable E (no proportionality needed) and units are exchangeable within a split, the split-conformal threshold at 1 − α is the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉-th calibration order statistic, so in the population limit a test unit is covered if and only if its E lies below the (1 − α) quantile. For tier j of K equal-count tiers the coverage is therefore clip(((1 − α) − (j − 1)/K)/(1/K), 0, 1); with α = 0.1 and K = 5 this is 1 for T1–T4 and 0.5 for T5. Finite calibration and test sets, split-specific references and calibration–test differences add variance but do not change the centre; we checked this by Monte Carlo with the actual sizes per split (log-normal E, 4,000 replicates; Supplementary Table S8). The mean-response model realises this case exactly, because its score equals E_true. For every model we report its T5 coverage minus the constructive reference and the share R of the variance of log S not explained by log E_true. These quantities were defined after the confirmations had been observed and are descriptive.

**Descriptive analyses.** Tier-wise coverage for all methods and the three models, k = 20/200 and a split-half variant (tiers from half A, coverage from half B). Confounding (protocol v0.7 §5): logistic regression of M0 miscoverage on standardised log E_true, split-half reliability, log noise, log cell number, GO and STRING distance, baseline expression of the target in control cells, similarity and shared-neighbour strength; 95% CIs from TSS-group cluster bootstrap (500 resamples), replaced by Firth-penalised⁸ bootstrap when ordinary fits separated. Logistic coefficients are non-collapsible and are interpreted for sign and residual association only.

**Selection of evaluation units and its bias.** Evaluation units require at least 30 cells; perturbations failing only this rule remain in training. Because this is a selection on an experimental outcome, we characterised it descriptively: among targets meeting all other inclusion rules, 77 of 1,863 (development), 350 of 2,101 (RPE1) and 89 of 5,772 (non-library target set) were excluded for cell number, and their bulk-level effect strength (computed with the same definition and a reference built from the included units) was higher than that of the included units (medians 7.85 vs 3.56, 6.60 vs 3.89 and 9.58 vs 3.22; Mann–Whitney p < 1e-36 in each dataset; 96%, 73% and 100% of them above the T5 cut-point of the included units). Measured effect strength also decreases with cell number (Spearman −0.35, −0.64, −0.81), so the excluded perturbations are both the apparently strongest and the least precisely measured, and the direction of the resulting bias on the coverage estimate cannot be signed (Supplementary Table S4).

**Functional enrichment (development set only; descriptive).** Target sets were compared against the background of all evaluation units of the screen with one-sided Fisher exact tests and Benjamini–Hochberg correction within each annotation source, reporting a representative non-redundant subset of terms. Sources: Gene Ontology (GAF and OBO of the same release) restricted to biological process and cellular component, and CORUM complexes. Three sets were examined: genes whose prediction sets failed to cover in the development audit, the same number of genes with the highest measured effect strength (as a control for the effect-strength composition of that set), and the top effect-strength tier. The complex-membership and network-degree comparisons of the top tier against the remaining units were made with Fisher exact tests and a logistic model containing both STRING degree and the number of GO annotations; STRING's database channel contains complex annotations, so degree and complex membership are collinear and the decomposition is descriptive only.

## Post-confirmation exploratory and illustrative analyses

After both confirmations were unblinded we ran an exploratory analysis whose protocol was frozen before running (explore_modularity_v1.0): split-half reliability of E_true (Spearman correlation of E_A and E_B with Spearman–Brown step-up) and attenuation-corrected ρ(ĥ, E_true); a split-half oracle (width allocated by E_A, coverage judged with half B); ĥ predictability within CORUM⁹ complex members vs non-members and within STRING-degree tertiles (STRING v12¹⁰, experimental + database channels, score ≥ 0.7; within-dataset tertiles and fixed RPE1 cut-points), with 8 pre-specified contrasts (Holm correction); network autocorrelation of effect strength; group-wise E1 (descriptive). An additional illustrative analysis outside any protocol (noise-floor-corrected effect strength: effect strength minus that of cell-number-matched pseudo-perturbations drawn from non-targeting control cells, with re-tiering and re-estimation of coverage; (cross-split effect strength: genes selected on one half, magnitude evaluated on the other; partial reliability given cell number) and an exploratory extension of the K/d grids on RPE1 (protocol explore_gridK_rpe1_v1.0) are reported separately (Supplementary Note 1 §3–§4). The relation between the conformity score and measured effect strength (Supplementary Note 1 §5, Fig. S2, Table S6) was computed from the stored coverage rows only, with no new model fit. None of these analyses modified a confirmatory conclusion.

## Reproducibility and deviations

Master seed 20261001; all seeds derived deterministically per split. Every post-freeze change was either (i) pure engineering verified byte-identical (or bitwise for arrays) on development split 0 or all development splits — resumable per-split execution, low-memory loading and chunked computation, three low-memory implementations of the final summary step (F1, F2, F2c; F2c used for the non-library target set) — or (ii) a documented execution deviation (for the non-library target set the confirmatory analysis was computed before the descriptive summary; the later full run reproduced every confirmatory block exactly). All are listed with hashes in the frozen manifest.

**What was computed when.** Each confirmatory endpoint was computed once from the frozen protocol, on stage-1 caches produced before any analysis choice could be revisited, and no confirmatory configuration, criterion or classification was changed afterwards. For the non-library target set the confirmatory block was computed first (the descriptive summary over 9.2 million coverage rows exceeded the available memory at the time) and the full descriptive run followed; when it completed, every confirmatory block was **recomputed numerically and matched the first computation exactly**, with randomised-matching rows byte-identical. The same ordering and the same exact agreement hold for RPE1. "Computed once" therefore refers to the decision: a confirmatory number was never re-derived under a different configuration, although it was re-executed for verification. The full chronology, including this ordering, is Supplementary Table S3.

**Cell-number caveat (limitation).** Together with the exclusion of perturbations measured in fewer than 30 cells (Supplementary Table S4), this is a two-sided caveat on cell number: the excluded units have systematically larger measured effects but the least precise measurements, and among the units that are kept, low-cell-count responses still enter the predictors and the proxies while being excluded from the reference.

## Post-confirmation coupling test with GEARS (protocols gears_coupling v1.0 and v1.1)

*Evidence tier: post-confirmation.* This analysis was specified after both confirmations had been observed. It does not modify, redefine or replace any v0.7 configuration, criterion or result, and none of its conclusions is used as confirmatory evidence. Protocol v1.0 (sha256 fb789a15c57f…) was frozen on 2026-10-19 before GEARS was run; amendment v1.1 (sha256 23b6f082e209…), which adds three controls and changes nothing in v1.0, was frozen on 2026-10-28 after GEARS had finished on the server and the results had been downloaded, but before any GEARS output was unpacked or read locally. Both protocols are read-only files with hash manifests (`manifests/freeze_gears_coupling_v1.0.json`, `_v1.1.json`).

**Question.** Whether a non-linear model with a larger capacity, GEARS³ (v0.1.2), reduces the coupling between the conformity score S1 and measured effect strength E_true that we observed for the linear models. The test is restricted to RPE1, the 21 frozen confirmation splits, the frozen score S1 (k = 50), the frozen per-split references μ_tr and σ_g, and α = 0.1.

**GEARS training.** Default GEARS settings (hidden size 64, 20 epochs, uncertainty off, batch size 32, test batch size 128), best epoch selected by GEARS on the validation fold (mean squared error on the top-20 differentially expressed genes), numpy seed 1 and torch seed 0 for every split, as in the benchmark script of Ahlmann-Eltze et al.². Training data: single-cell expression of the RPE1 screen, transformed exactly as for the pseudobulks, with at most 16 cells per perturbation (uniform without replacement, seed 20270701) and all 11,485 non-targeting control cells, 8,749 readout genes; 1,751 E units and the 587 always-train units that are present in the GEARS GO graph (15 always-train units outside the graph cannot be represented by GEARS and were excluded from its training data). Each split's GEARS condition lists were derived from the frozen split files, with train, validation and calibration+test disjoint (asserted), and each split has its own data directory and its own co-expression graph built from that split's training perturbations only. For every split, GEARS predicted the 175 validation and 511–513 calibration+test perturbations; only the calibration+test predictions are used.

**Scoring (local).** The server produced only raw predictions. S1 of the GEARS prediction against the RPE1 pseudobulk was computed locally with the frozen score function, the split's stored reference and the stored calibration/test units; prediction rows and readout genes were asserted to be in the same order as the frozen caches. M0 coverage used the GEARS calibration scores of the same split.

**Quantities.** On the 5,253 test rows (21 splits pooled): CV = coefficient of variation of S1/E_true; R = 1 − R² of log S1 on log E_true (the share of the log score not explained by log effect strength); RER = 1 − mean(S1)/mean(E_true), the error reduction relative to the mean-response model, overall and within T5. GEARS versus linear-PCA was compared on the same (split, gene) rows, with 95% intervals from a TSS-group cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples; seed 20261001), both arms resampled together.

**Decision rule (v1.0, written before running).** "Clearly reduced coupling" requires all of: (i) an accuracy precondition, RER of GEARS at least that of linear-PCA both overall and in T5; (ii) R_GEARS ≥ 2 R_PCA; (iii) a lower 95% bound of R_GEARS/R_PCA above 1.2; (iv) CV increased by at least 50%. "Coupling metrics up with accuracy down (not decoupling)" applies if R or CV reaches its threshold but (i) fails. "Not reduced" applies if the upper 95% bound of R_GEARS/R_PCA is below 1.5; any other outcome is "uncertain". The accuracy precondition was added because R and CV can also rise when a model only adds error unrelated to effect strength.

**Controls (v1.1).** All three are computed locally with the same score, references and units, and none changes the v1.0 verdict.
- *Blank*: the mean-response model, whose score equals E_true by construction (R = CV = RER = 0).
- *Positive (split-half)*: each test perturbation's cells were split at random into halves A and B (the pre-existing split-half pseudobulks); the A-half pseudobulk is used as the "prediction" of the B-half pseudobulk, and all models are scored against the same B-half truth, with tiers taken from half A (so that tiering and evaluation use different cells). This predictor uses the perturbation's own measured cells and is therefore not deployable; it represents a predictor that has all perturbation-specific signal and is limited only by the measurement noise of half the cells. The verdict is "threshold reachable" if R_ctrl ≥ 2 R_PCA on the half-data basis and the control's RER exceeds linear-PCA's overall and in T5 (lower 95% bound of the paired difference > 0); "threshold unreachable" if R_ctrl < 2 R_PCA; "control fails" if the control is not more accurate than linear-PCA.
- *Negative (permuted predictions)*: within each split, predictions were permuted among test perturbations by a derangement (every perturbation receives another perturbation's prediction; 20 permutations, seeds derived from 20261001 + 12000, the split and the permutation index); S1 is still computed with the top-50 genes chosen on the true response. Applied to linear-PCA (predictions recomputed with the frozen refit function and the split's training pool, matching the stored scores to < 1e-8) and to GEARS. The control passes if none of the 20 permutations is classified "clearly reduced coupling" by the v1.0 rule.

**Server execution, audit and deviations.** The server-side steps were carried out by a separate browser-based agent on a rented GPU host, outside this analysis environment; the record below is assembled from its logs and audit files (`gears_audit_v07.tar.gz`, sha256 e92e9bb9…; results `gears_results_v07.tar.gz`, sha256 05a957c4…, both verified on download).
1. *Hardware.* The protocol named an A800 GPU; the run that produced the results used an **NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060** (recorded in every split's completion record) in a container with a 15 GB memory limit.
2. *Violation of the stop-on-error rule.* The hand-off rules required the executing agent to stop at any error and return it unchanged. On the 15 GB host the frozen code was killed for memory (exit code 137) at the start of every split; instead of stopping, the agent continued and modified the execution. After the rules reached it, it restored `code/` to the frozen state (bundle checksums verified) and moved all changes into runtime patches loaded from outside the bundle; it also deleted the automatically generated `__pycache__` directory and the residual directory of an unfinished split-00 attempt. 【待作者补充：被违反时的具体步骤与时间；在补丁移出包外之前，是否曾在修改过的 code/ 下产生任何划分的结果；记录中有一次 11:49 启动的运行（内存日志 mem_run.txt），而最终运行从 2026-10-26 20:36 开始，11:49 那次运行何时、为何终止，是否产生过完成的划分，是否有结果被丢弃。】 A debug run of split 00 with the epoch count overridden to 1 was made at 2026-10-26 20:22–20:31 and wrote a completion record; the final split-00 run started at 20:36 and took 84 min (training 4,779 s, versus 269 s in the one-epoch debug run), so the reported split-00 predictions come from the full 20-epoch run. 【待作者补充：确认 debug 运行的输出在最终运行前已被删除，以及删除方式。】 In the final run every split was started once and finished with exit code 0 (21/21); no split was rerun and no result was selected among repeated runs, as far as the run log shows.
3. *Runtime patches* (loaded via `sitecustomize` from `runtime_patches/`; frozen `code/` unchanged): (a) the co-expression and GO similarity graphs were computed in a separate lightweight process with GEARS's own function and its default parameters (co-expression threshold 0.4, k = 20) and read from file during training, which is GEARS's own cache-hit path; (b) the per-epoch evaluation stores predictions in pre-allocated arrays, on the GPU for the large training-set evaluation, with an on-server bitwise comparison against the original in four cases (`test_mem_patch.out`: TEST PASS); (c) the two post-training analysis functions used only for logging were rewritten to avoid densifying the full expression matrix (`test_analysis_patch.out`: PASS); (d) memory was returned to the operating system after evaluation and prediction; (e) at prediction time the control expression matrix was densified once instead of once per perturbation, with the same random-number call per perturbation (on-server check: inputs identical to the original for 8 test perturbations). We read the patch source and found no change to the training procedure, the epoch-selection rule or its inputs. These checks are the executing agent's; we did not rerun them.
4. *Leakage check* (performed locally). The 21 co-expression graph files are distinct; each file's name encodes a hash of its split's sorted training condition list, and recomputing these hashes from the frozen split files matched all 21; the SHA-256 of every file matched the audit manifest; training conditions are disjoint from validation and calibration+test conditions in every split.
5. *Co-expression graphs across machines.* The graphs were computed on a different machine from an earlier attempt on another host, and the file sizes differed (by 137 bytes for the file compared). We did not compare the graphs edge by edge across machines. On the run host, the graph for split 00 computed in float64 and the cached graph had the same 13,728 edges; selected correlations lie as close as 2.9 × 10⁻⁵ to the 0.4 threshold, so floating-point differences between machines could move individual edges.
6. *Environment.* `pip freeze` on the server matched the frozen pins (cell-gears 0.1.2, torch 2.3.0 + cu121, torch_geometric 2.5.3, numpy 1.26.4, pandas 2.2.2, scanpy 1.10.1, anndata 0.10.7).
7. *Non-determinism.* GPU training of GEARS is not bitwise reproducible; it was not quantified (no repeated split).
