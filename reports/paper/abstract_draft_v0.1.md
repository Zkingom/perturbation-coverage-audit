# Abstract (draft v0.1, 2026-10-18)

Machine-learning models of single-cell perturbation responses are increasingly used to decide which perturbations to test, yet their uncertainty statements are usually validated only on average. We audited the conditional validity of split-conformal prediction sets for perturbation-response models in genome-scale CRISPRi Perturb-seq screens, treating the target gene as the exchangeable unit and holding out whole transcription-start-site groups. Marginal coverage was at its nominal level in all three screens, but coverage of the 20% of perturbations with the strongest measured effects collapsed to about half: 0.48 (95% CI 0.43–0.53; 1,751 genes) in a pre-registered confirmation in a second cell line (RPE1) and 0.52 (95% CI 0.49–0.55; 5,683 genes) in a pre-registered confirmation on target genes outside the essential-gene library, while the four weaker quintiles were covered almost always. Six deployable difficulty proxies based on similarity to the training data, gene-network distance or model uncertainty gave no improvement in either confirmation set. Because the conformity score is close to a multiple of the measured effect, repairing conditional coverage requires predicting the scale of a perturbation's response before the experiment. A deployable proxy built from the measured responses of prior-knowledge neighbours met the pre-registered criteria on RPE1 (top-quintile coverage +9.7 pp (95% CI 5.8 to 13.6) at matched interval width) but failed them on the target genes outside the library (+1.0 pp (95% CI -0.1 to 2.1); the pre-registered margin was 5 pp), where it barely predicted effect strength (Spearman ρ = 0.11). Effect-strength-stratified coverage is therefore a necessary component of uncertainty audits for perturbation models, and experiment-free prediction of perturbation scale is the limiting step for repairing it.
