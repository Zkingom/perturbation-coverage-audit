# Introduction (draft v0.2, 2026-10-28)

Pooled CRISPR screens read out by single-cell RNA sequencing now measure the transcriptional consequences of thousands of genetic perturbations in one experiment¹, and a growing family of models predicts the response of perturbations that were not measured²⁻⁴. The practical promise of such models is triage: deciding which of many candidate perturbations to run next. That use requires more than an accurate point prediction — it requires knowing which predictions can be trusted.

The field's assessment of predictive performance has become considerably more careful. Linear baselines match or exceed deep models on standard benchmarks when the evaluation is set up carefully², the choice of reference point determines whether an apparent effect is perturbation-specific or a systematic offset shared by all perturbations⁴, and a recent systematic comparison showed that model rankings depend strongly on the size of a perturbation's effect and recommended effect-size-stratified evaluation⁵. These advances concern the accuracy of the prediction. They do not say how an uncertainty statement attached to that prediction should be validated.

Conformal prediction offers a distribution-free way to attach prediction sets with a finite-sample coverage guarantee⁶. The guarantee, however, is marginal: it holds on average over the exchangeable units, and says nothing about any identifiable subgroup. In a screen, the subgroups that matter are exactly the ones a biologist would act on — the perturbations with the largest effects, or those least similar to anything measured before. Methods that restore coverage conditional on a covariate exist⁷, and work on perturbation prediction under distribution shift has concentrated on similarity to the training data, including an uncertainty-aware method that calibrates against the difficulty of each perturbation⁸. What has not been established is how a conditional-coverage audit of these screens should be read: whether marginal conformal calibration fails for identifiable perturbations, how much of any such failure is fixed by the way subgroups are defined rather than by the model, and whether a deployable method changes it — in a setting where the answer is tested once on held-out data rather than selected on the same data that produced it.

We report such an audit. Our contribution is not a new conformal algorithm and not a new perturbation model: it is a perturbation-level audit of conditional coverage, a way to read it, and one pre-registered transfer test. Specifically, we (i) treat the target gene, not the cell, as the exchangeable unit and hold out whole transcription-start-site groups so that CRISPRi repression of a neighbouring promoter cannot leak between folds; (ii) fix every analysis choice on a development screen and freeze it, with hashes, before any response of either confirmation screen enters a score; and (iii) state in advance what would count as a failure, as a repair, and as a repair that adds to the best distance-based alternative. The two confirmation screens were chosen to separate two kinds of generalisation: a second cell line with largely the same target genes, and a disjoint set of target genes in the same cell line.

Coverage of the strongest perturbations was about one half in every screen. We show that, for current predictors, this is largely determined by construction: the conformity score is close to a multiple of the measured effect, and for such a score quantile-stratified coverage is fixed in advance. We therefore give a constructive reference and a diagnostic — the share of the score that is independent of effect scale — against which stratified coverage should be read, extending effect-size-stratified evaluation⁵ to uncertainty. In a test specified after the confirmations, a non-linear model, GEARS³, was more accurate than the linear baseline without detectably changing this share, while a measurement-limited positive control showed that the share can be raised. A deployable proxy for effect scale built from prior-knowledge neighbours met the pre-registered criteria across cell lines and failed them on the disjoint gene set; we report the negative confirmation as pre-specified, because the conditions under which such a repair transfers are what a practitioner needs to know.
