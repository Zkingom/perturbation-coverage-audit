# Discussion (draft v0.7, 2026-10-28)

**What stratified coverage measures.** Across three Perturb-seq screens, split-conformal prediction sets for linear perturbation models were marginally valid yet covered only about half of the 20% strongest perturbations and almost all weaker ones. Most of this pattern is fixed before any data are seen: when the conformity score is an increasing function of the variable used to define the tiers, a single global quantile covers T1–T4 completely and T5 with probability one half, and the fitted models stayed within about 1.5 percentage points of that constructive reference. For a perturbation model, effect strength is that kind of variable whenever the predictor captures little perturbation-specific signal, because the score then reduces to the size of the perturbation's own response. The audit is therefore informative mainly through how far a model departs from the constructive reference, which we summarise by the share R of the score that is not explained by effect scale. This bears directly on the recommendation of Li et al.¹¹ to evaluate perturbation models stratified by effect size: such stratification is useful, but when the predictor captures little specific signal, effect-stratified coverage is largely determined by construction, and the constructive reference should be reported alongside it so that the remaining, model-specific part can be read. The relevant regime — the strongest predicted perturbations, which are the ones a practitioner would prioritise — is exactly where a marginal guarantee says least.

**A more accurate non-linear model did not reduce the coupling detectably.** GEARS reduced the mean-model error more than linear-PCA, overall and in the top tier, but its scale-independent share R was not larger (ratio 0.91, 95% CI 0.76 to 1.11), and its top-tier coverage was 0.498, at the constructive reference. This is an absence of evidence for a reduction with an interval that includes no change, not evidence that the coupling cannot be reduced. The positive control shows that it can be, in principle: predicting each perturbation's profile from its own other half of the cells gave R = 0.83 on the half-data basis, against 0.039 for linear-PCA and 0.036 for GEARS on the same basis. That control uses the perturbation's own measurements and is out of reach for any prediction of an unseen perturbation; it marks how much scale-independent variation a measurement-noise-limited predictor with full specific signal would show, and so the gap between it and both models quantifies how far current predictors are from that level, not which signal GEARS misses. The negative control confirms that the decision rule does not mistake perturbation-unrelated error for decoupling (20/20 linear-PCA and 19/20 GEARS permutations "not reduced"). As an observation outside the pre-specified analyses, GEARS predictions permuted among perturbations still reduced the mean-model error by 3.4% (linear-PCA 0.6%), suggesting that part of GEARS's accuracy gain is not perturbation-specific.

**Distance-based proxies did not capture this axis.** Out-of-distribution work on perturbation prediction emphasises how far a test perturbation is from the training data. Six deployable proxies built on that idea — GO and STRING distances, similarity- and data-quality-based difficulty, and a bootstrap model-uncertainty proxy in the spirit of PRESCRIBE — produced no T5 gain on either confirmation set under width matching, and in confounding analyses distance had no residual association with miscoverage for the primary model once effect strength was accounted for. Effect strength, by contrast, remained the dominant predictor in every dataset. These are six specific proxies, one score and two linear models, so the finding is that distance did not capture the failure axis here, not that distance is irrelevant in general. We nonetheless suggest that calibration audits of perturbation models report coverage stratified by effect strength in addition to similarity-based OOD splits; consistent with this, a recent benchmark found that model rankings depend strongly on perturbation effect size and recommended effect-size-stratified evaluation¹¹, although its direction of dependence varied with the metric and it did not address uncertainty quantification.

**A prior-neighbour proxy transferred across cell lines but not across gene sets.** Because the score is close to a multiple of effect strength, a deployable method can change stratified coverage only with information about each perturbation's scale obtained before the experiment. Our one proxy, built from GO and STRING neighbours' measured effects, passed the pre-registered test on RPE1 (E1 +9.7 pp, 95% CI 5.8 to 13.6) and failed it on the non-library target set (E1 +1.0 pp, 95% CI −0.1 to 2.1; post hoc scenario power 0.96 for half the development-set effect at the observed SE), in line with its predictability (ρ = 0.43 vs 0.11). Subsets defined by the proxy before the experiment under-covered by the amount their effect-strength composition predicts, so they add no evidence beyond the construction. Post-confirmation analyses suggest a role for network structure: effect strength was autocorrelated on STRING among essential genes but hardly among non-library targets, and highly connected essential genes were covered somewhat better than their composition predicts. A possible reading is that essential genes act through dense, co-regulated machines, so that a gene's effect is predictable from its partners; this is a hypothesis generated after unblinding on subsets we chose, not a finding.

**The repair has a cost.** Normalising by ĥ at equal total width moves width from perturbations predicted to be weak to those predicted to be strong. Perturbations with little prior information pay for this: in the lowest STRING-degree tertile, the change in T5 coverage under M1 was negative in all three datasets, with CIs excluding zero on RPE1 and non-library target set (RPE1 -14.9 pp (95% CI -22.4 to -7.5); non-library target set -3.7 pp (95% CI -5.5 to -1.8); development -5.1 pp (95% CI -15.8 to 5.5)), whereas the gains concentrated in densely connected genes (non-library target set, high-degree stratum at fixed cut-points, +10.2 pp (95% CI 6.0 to 14.3)). These group-wise numbers are descriptive and were not corrected for the nine groups examined. A practitioner should not apply the repair uniformly. One natural extension — enabling the repair only where the proxy is expected to be predictive, for example by network degree — is left to future work; we did not construct or test such a rule on the present data, because any rule tuned here would need new data for a valid confirmation.

**Limitations.**
1. *Circularity.* ĥ is built from neighbours' effect strengths, so "low network autocorrelation" and "low ĥ predictability" overlap conceptually and the statement that one explains the other is partly tautological. The information that is not circular is threefold: the split-half reliability of effect strength (≈ 0.99 in all datasets) and its cross-split version make noise in the outcome an unlikely sole explanation for the low predictability on the non-library target set, though reliability counts any component shared by both cell halves as signal and therefore cannot exclude it; the stratifying effect of network position (degree, complex membership) on predictability and repair is consistent across three datasets; and a cross-dataset gap persists within matched degree strata.
2. *Reliability is not validity.* Split-half reliability measures repeatability across cell halves; components shared by both halves (for example a cell-number-dependent noise floor in the top-gene selection, or batch) count as signal. An illustrative cross-split analysis (genes selected on one half, magnitude measured on the other) left ĥ's predictability unchanged (non-library target set, ρ = 0.119) and reliability stayed high after partialling out cell number (non-library target set, 0.960). However, on the non-library target set measured effect strength is strongly anti-correlated with cell number (ρ = -0.80), so part of the strong-effect tier there may reflect measurement precision (a noise floor) rather than biological effect size; this cannot be separated from fitness effects on cell yield with the present data (Supplementary Note 1 §3). In a post hoc illustrative analysis, on both essential-gene screens, re-tiering perturbations by effect strength corrected for a cell-number-matched null from non-targeting control cells left the failure essentially unchanged (M0 T5 coverage 0.55 and 0.49); on non-library target set the same correction raised T5 coverage from 0.52 to 0.66 and 21% of perturbations did not exceed the null, so on the non-library target set a sizeable part of the apparent failure reflects the precision of low-cell-number measurements. Reporting coverage by noise-corrected effect strength should accompany any such audit.
3. *Study bias.* STRING degree and CORUM membership partly reflect how intensively a gene has been studied, not only its biological modularity; our analyses cannot separate the two.
4. *Selection of evaluation units.* Perturbations with fewer than 30 cells are excluded from evaluation, and these have systematically higher measured effect strength than the included ones (medians 7.9 vs 3.6, 6.6 vs 3.9 and 9.6 vs 3.2; 73–100% above the T5 cut-point of the included units). The audit is therefore performed after truncating the apparently strongest tail. Since measured effect strength also rises as cell number falls, these perturbations are simultaneously the least precisely measured, and the direction of the resulting bias cannot be signed with these data. Conversely, among the units that are kept, the cell-number floor applies to the reference and the mean model but not to the linear models' fitting columns or to the proxy's neighbour features, so low-cell-number responses enter the predictors while being excluded from the reference (Methods).
5. *Scope of the confirmation.* All three screens come from one laboratory and platform, and two are in K562; most RPE1 targets overlap the development targets, so RPE1 tests generalisation across cell lines for largely the same genes, while non-library target set tests generalisation to new genes. Cross-laboratory replication (e.g. in recently published perturbation atlases¹²) is needed.
6. *Hyper-parameter grids.* The selected K and d often lay at the upper end of the pre-specified grids; in an exploratory extension on RPE1 (K up to 200, d up to 200) the failure and the partial repair were unchanged (M0 T5 0.49; M1 − M0 +9.5 [5.6, 13.4] pp), although d still sat at the new ceiling in every split and validation error improved by < 2% (Supplementary Note 1 §4).
7. *Choices of score and unit.* Results are for a perturbation-level score on perturbation-specific genes relative to the training centroid; other scores (per-gene intervals, differential-expression calls) may behave differently.
8. *The GEARS test is one model on one dataset.* It covers a single non-linear model (GEARS v0.1.2 with default settings) on RPE1 only, with training data capped at 16 cells per perturbation by protocol and 15 always-train perturbations outside the GEARS GO graph excluded from its training; GEARS's accuracy under this cap may be lower than with all cells. "No detected reduction" in this setting does not show that other or larger models would leave the coupling unchanged.
9. *Deviations in the GEARS run* (Methods, *Server execution, audit and deviations*). (a) The run used an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060, not the A800 named in the protocol. (b) The stop-on-error rule was violated: after out-of-memory failures on a 15 GB host, the executing agent continued and changed the execution instead of stopping, later moving all changes into five runtime patches outside the frozen bundle; we audited the patch source and found no change to training or epoch selection, but the equivalence tests were run by that agent on the server and were not repeated by us. The violation occurred after all 21 splits had been killed for memory on 2026-10-25; the subsequent failed attempts, including a run started at 11:49 on 2026-10-26, all ended before writing any completion record, and in the final run every split ran once (21/21, exit code 0), so no output of a failed attempt entered the results and no result was selected among attempts. (c) A one-epoch debug run of split 00 preceded the final run; it wrote to a separate directory and did not enter the reported results. (d) Co-expression graphs were computed on a different machine from an earlier attempt and were not compared edge by edge across machines; some correlations lie within 3 × 10⁻⁵ of the graph threshold, so floating-point differences could change individual edges. (e) GPU non-determinism of GEARS training was not quantified.
10. *Post-confirmation status of the coupling analyses.* The constructive reference, the scale-independent share R, the GEARS test and its controls were defined after the confirmations had been observed; they are interpretive and post-confirmation, not confirmatory.

**Conclusion.** Under current perturbation-response predictors, coverage stratified by measured effect strength is largely determined by the quantile construction, because the conformity score is close to a multiple of effect scale. Audits of uncertainty for perturbation models should report the constructive reference and the scale-independent share of the score alongside stratified coverage. In our data neither a linear nor a non-linear predictor departed detectably from the constructive case, although a measurement-limited control shows that departure is possible; a prior-network proxy for scale transferred across cell lines within essential genes but not to a set of targets outside that library.
